01
Foundations

Understanding AI today

Generative AI reads and writes language. Given a clear instruction, it drafts text, summarises long documents, translates between languages, answers questions, and turns rough notes into structured output. It works in seconds and it works at scale. That is the source of its value, and the reason it now sits inside the daily tools that officials already use.

Where it is strong

Language work at speed

  • Drafting letters, briefs, slides, and speaking notes from an outline
  • Summarising long reports, appeals, and correspondence
  • Translation between Azerbaijani and other languages
  • Comparing a document against best practice or a standard
  • Preparing for meetings and tracking actions
Where judgement stays human

It assists, it does not decide

  • It can state something incorrect with full confidence
  • It struggles with nuanced policy analysis and sensitive cases
  • It has no authority and carries no accountability
  • Outputs must be checked against an authoritative source
  • Decisions of consequence remain with named officials

The practical conclusion is simple. AI is a capable assistant for the language heavy parts of government work, and a poor substitute for the judgement, verification, and accountability that leadership provides. The rest of this guide is about capturing the first while protecting the second.

02
The three environments

Three ways to use AI, with very different risk

The same underlying model can be reached through three very different doors. The door you choose decides who can see the information you type, whether there is an audit trail, and which class of government data may be used at all. The safe path today is a governed enterprise account, not a consumer chatbot.

Not for official work

Personal account

A private login on a public tool, used outside any agreement with the entity.

  • Prompts may be retained and used to train the model
  • No central control, no audit trail
  • Suitable only for public, non official information
The starting point

Enterprise account

An account contracted by the entity, with agreed terms and central administration.

  • The model does not train on your data
  • Central administration, access control, and audit
  • Data is still processed on the vendor cloud
For the most sensitive work

Fully secure environment

A closed platform on state premises, where data never leaves national control.

  • No dependence on an external cloud
  • Accredited for confidential and restricted data
  • Higher cost and effort to establish and run

What this means. Adoption should begin with an enterprise account that enables secure, auditable, and compliant use of AI for official work. The most sensitive data waits for a closed environment built for the purpose.

Three principles that more than fourteen governments now share

The OECD finds that governments experimenting with generative AI converge on the same three rules. Each is simple to state and consequential in practice.

Classify the data first

The data classification tier decides the permitted environment, and with it the tasks that may be performed.

Verify every output

Check each output against an authoritative source before it informs a decision, an official record, or advice to leadership.

Disclose AI involvement

State clearly when AI has materially shaped a public facing output, so accountability stays with the entity.

Source: OECD, Generative AI experimentation in government (2026).

04
Deep dive

The enterprise account, in practice

This is where adoption begins. Inside a governed enterprise account, officials can safely use AI for public and internal work. The value concentrates in four families of task. For each, there is a clear line between what to do and what to avoid, drawn by the sensitivity of the data involved.

General use

Question and answer, drafting, translation, meeting preparation

Do   Translate a cleared public speech or press line from Azerbaijani into English.

Avoid   Preparing the President's meeting pack from ministry submissions and recommended decisions.

Productivity

Summaries, inbox, administration, action tracking

Do   Design a template tracker for initiative control.

Avoid   Summarising a citizen appeal that contains identity, case history, and routing.

Content generation

Slides, speaking notes, reports

Do   Draft a slide pack on a national programme using published statistics and international comparators.

Avoid   Speaking notes for a closed session on a decree still in inter agency circulation.

Research

Best practice, cross checking, synthesis

Do   Analyse OECD and EU best practice on policy design.

Avoid   Comparing a confidential ministry package against those same standards.

Example prompts you can use today

Good prompts state the role, the task, the source material, and the format. These use only public or internal material, and every output is checked before use.

Translation
You are a government translator. Translate the following cleared public statement from Azerbaijani into formal English suited to an international audience. Keep the tone measured and preserve official titles exactly.
Briefing from public sources
Summarise this published national strategy into a one page brief for a senior official. Give three headline points, the main commitments, and two open questions worth raising. Use plain language and cite the section for each point.
Comparison against best practice
Compare our published policy approach against OECD best practice on the same topic. Present the result as a table with three columns: our approach, the OECD practice, and the gap. Note where our approach is already ahead.
Meeting preparation
From these public agenda items, draft a short preparation note for the chair. For each item give the decision required, the likely points of debate, and one clarifying question.
United Kingdom · case study

A trial across 20,000 officials saved 26 minutes a day, and governance decided the value

The UK Government Digital Service ran the largest government trial of an AI assistant to date, deploying Microsoft 365 Copilot to 20,000 officials across twelve departments, including tax, welfare, justice, foreign affairs, and statistics, over three months in late 2024.

What decided the outcome. Governance came before rollout, because the tool inherits each user's permissions, so departments fixed data access first. Value concentrated in drafting. Human oversight stayed mandatory for policy analysis and sensitive cases. Reaching sustained use was a change programme built on training.

26 minsaved per official per day
85%said it gave good value
82%would choose to keep using it
~80%reached sustained active use

Source: UK Government Digital Service, Microsoft 365 Copilot cross government findings report (June 2025).

Defence organisation

A digital twin for investment planning

The challenge. Delays in mission readiness stretched over several years, while manual planning absorbed the time of large military and industry teams and exposed them to avoidable risk.

The outcome. A digital twin made gaps in the target force structure transparent and sequenced capability investments. The whole of defence campaign plan became a live scenario that could be adapted as inventories changed.

2xfaster mission planning
5xfaster budget rationalisation
Public sector services agency

A regulatory synthesiser for inspectors

The challenge. Inspectors analysed regulatory standards through unstructured online searches, which risked misinformation and delay. Requirements were often recalled from memory and could be missed.

The outcome. A proof of concept summarised regulatory documents with their sources, shaped by user sessions and testing, with a plan to add national language support and widen coverage.

~1,300hours saved per month

Government agency, more than 30,000 staff

One tracker to prioritise initiatives

The challenge. A large agency distributing sizeable citizen payments ran many uncoordinated initiatives, with no clear view of what actually improved service delivery.

The outcome. A single initiative tracker, outcome oriented metrics, and a prioritisation workshop stopped work that was off target and focused effort on outcomes leadership cared about.

1.4mnannualised workforce hours saved
+9%citizen satisfaction
Ministry

A live decision dashboard for leadership

The challenge. Facts sat in different departmental formats and were compiled by hand into a static twenty page report, with little clarity and heavy effort to update.

The outcome. An interactive dashboard with standardised data, personal views, exports, and updates from many contributors, built with leadership, chiefs of staff, and speechwriters.

1 sourceof truth for leadership facts

Public authority

Writing assistance for official correspondence

The challenge. The authority issues around 70 million documents a year, with individual letters formulated by hand from roughly 50,000 templates.

The outcome. An assistant identifies the laws relevant to each case from officer notes and drafts the correspondence, including legal references, with human sign off retained.

Cases designed for executive leadership

Executive assistant

Stronger meeting memory and follow through

AI prepares personalised briefing packs, synthesises meeting topics into recommended actions, and tracks directives from minutes, so decisions reach closure rather than generating more follow ups.

  • Up to 40% of meetings avoided entirely
  • Two hours of daily preparation time reduced

Building the platform

What a sovereign AI capability requires

A fully secure environment rests on a tightly integrated stack, from energy through to policy, underpinned by local ownership, operations, and governance. Each layer depends on the one beneath it. Sovereignty means the whole stack stays under national control.

Two ways to host the most sensitive work

Super classified work can run in either of two homes. They are not a choice of one against the other, but a sequence. Accredit the secure room first, then build the dedicated facility as data volumes and cleared staff grow.

DimensionSovereign platform on state premisesDedicated classified facility
FacilitiesAn accredited secure room inside an existing state data centreA purpose built classified facility with a backup site
TechnologyA vendor supplied secure AI platform on state infrastructureLarge scale compute clusters dedicated to classified AI
GovernanceThe state governs, the vendor operates and upgrades the platformThe state owns operations, governance, and recovery outright
PeopleA small technical team, with vendor knowledge transferA large cleared workforce across operations, security, and engineering
ResilienceRecovery built into the platformPrimary and secondary sites with failover
AssessmentLower cost, reuses existing facilities, shares infrastructure, ongoing vendor dependenceMaximum security and full sovereignty, highest investment, cleared specialists hard to recruit

Sequence, do not choose. Option one reaches production faster and reuses state facilities. Option two delivers full sovereignty but demands the largest investment. Start with the accredited secure room, and build the dedicated facility as volumes and clearances grow.

Four foundational enablers

Capturing value from the platform, rather than a single pilot, depends on four enablers advancing together.

01

AI talent and capability

  • Recruit and retain the talent to run compute workloads at scale
  • Raise operational maturity with defined roles, ownership, and runbooks
  • Transfer skills from vendors to civil servants through paired delivery
02

Data and technology foundations

  • Consolidate siloed legacy systems and curate high quality data
  • Implement network segmentation, identity management, and encryption
  • Harden environments so AI scales beyond isolated pilots
03

Operational AI governance

  • Embed guardrails, access controls, and validation of inputs and outputs
  • Issue usage guidance that states what officials may do in each environment
  • Monitor progress against outcome metrics, reviewed by leadership each quarter
04

Cost and financial sustainability

  • Size compute to a demand forecast and re plan as real usage emerges
  • Pool infrastructure across entities to avoid idle capacity
  • Track cost per use case and retire those that do not cover their running cost

Summary

Where to start

The path is deliberately staged. Begin with safe, governed use that delivers value in weeks, prove it, then build the secure national capability that unlocks the most sensitive work. Value arrives early and compounds as the platform matures.

  • The safe path today is a governed enterprise account, not a consumer chatbot.
  • Classification decides everything. The tier of the data sets the permitted environment and the tasks allowed.
  • Public and internal work can start now in the enterprise account, with verification and disclosure.
  • Sensitive and restricted work waits for the closed platform, then becomes the source of the largest gains.
  • Sequence the build. Accredit a secure room first, and grow into a dedicated facility as demand and clearances rise.

A sequenced path

  1. Weeks 1 to 8

    Stand up the governed enterprise account

    Contract an enterprise account, fix data access before rollout, and issue usage guidance covering the three principles. Fix the permissions first, as the UK trial shows this decides the value.

  2. Quarter 1

    Capture early value and build the habit

    Focus on drafting, translation, summaries, and meeting preparation on public and internal data. Train widely, since familiarity predicted the time saved. Measure minutes returned to officials.

  3. Quarters 1 to 2

    Accredit a secure room inside an existing state data centre

    Choose the sovereign platform on state premises to reach production faster. Establish governance, clearances, and the small technical team, with vendor knowledge transfer.

  4. Quarters 2 to 4

    Prove high value cases on sensitive data

    Deliver two or three flagship cases in the secure environment: a leadership decision dashboard, case correspondence drafting, and cross domain analysis for meetings.

  5. Year 2 and beyond

    Scale toward full sovereignty

    As volumes and cleared staff grow, invest in the dedicated classified facility and the wider sovereign stack, advancing the four enablers together and pooling infrastructure across entities.

The first step is small and safe. A governed enterprise account, clear guidance, and a short list of public and internal use cases can begin within weeks, while planning for the secure room runs in parallel.
