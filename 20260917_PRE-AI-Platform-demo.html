What changed, and what you are about to see

Two weeks ago this was a design. The risk has moved from building it to trusting it.

What exists now that did not two weeks ago

  • Three services deployed in Azure, all warm Portal, application core, model gateway. Every one shipped by the pipeline — nothing hand-deployed, so any of us can ship the next change.
  • The real portfolio, served from the database 32 PASHA developments with their CAPEX, across five RIBA stages, through the deployed chain.
  • A model gateway that no service can route around Authenticated by managed identity. No model API key exists anywhere in the estate.
  • The data schema at v2.0 40 tables, 501 columns, reviewed column by column and back with Faig on seven items.
  • CI and delivery back under our control Build minutes ran out on 15 September; a self-hosted runner inside our own network replaced them the same day.

The two proofs, live on the deployed system

Samir 7 minutes

The portfolio and one project, end to end. Search and filter run against the database while you watch — this is an application, not a screenshot. He will also show you the stages that are deliberately empty, because honest empty states are how the data actually looks today.

Madiyar 5 minutes

An agent run on that same project: the platform asks a real model to draft the project brief, and the draft comes back on screen with a verification hash beside it. Then the control that makes this safe — identifiers are masked before anything leaves our network, and restored on the way back.

What we are not claiming. The path to the model is proven; the quality of what it writes has nothing behind it yet. Building that evaluation is explicitly the next two weeks' work.

The next two weeks

The gate is reachable on engineering. The risks are quality, environment, and inputs we cannot produce ourselves.

Not a plan slide. Everything on the left has to be true on 30 September, and the four risks on the right are the reasons it might not be. Two of the four are inputs, not engineering.

What lands by 30 September

M1 · Stages 1–3 and the foundation

  • Stage 1 writeable end to end Create a project, capture its intent, produce a brief — and come back to the same one tomorrow.
  • The 40-table schema applied to the live database Turns on Faig closing out seven review items this week.
  • Stages 2 and 3 reading live data The screens and the estimate structure, on whatever inputs exist by then.
  • Gateway and agentic hardening, plus repository governance A red build stops being able to reach the main branch by omission.
  • The first evaluation of answer quality So that next time we can say the answer is good, not only that the call succeeded.

The four risks we are carrying

  • Answer quality is unproven The plumbing works and nothing yet judges the output. Until an evaluation exists, no AI-drafted result should be treated as reviewed.
  • Stage 3’s BoQ has no way into the platform In the BRD, five of the seven BoQ intake steps are performed by a third party inside the platform. That needs a protected public entry point and a test environment to prove it in, and we have neither. Until then a BoQ arrives as an email attachment and someone re-keys it.
  • … and no agreed format for it to land in The unified BoQ item list does not exist, and only PRE can supply it. It is inside this gate, and three other pieces of work key off it — ask 1.
  • Concept Design cost data has to be typed in by hand, project by project Stages 1–2 estimate from realised costs, and that data exists in nobody’s system — the business supplies it manually per project. Concept Design is the harder of the two and its format is not defined until end of September. Even once the format lands, the capture is human effort nobody has been resourced for, so this is the risk most likely to be slow rather than blocked.
Next review 1 October — the first one after the gate What we expect to show then: Stage 1 end to end on the real schema, a number for answer quality, and the first real users on it

After the gate

The MVP is not a bigger demo. It is the first time someone outside this team creates a real project.

Straight after 30 September. A small group of PRE users get their own accounts and run their own projects on the platform. The point is feedback from real work, not a wider audience.

What a real user will do

  • Sign in as themselves, with their own role and their own projects
  • Create a project and capture what it is meant to be
  • Get an AI-drafted brief, then correct it and keep the correction
  • See their own portfolio and their own worklist
  • Come back the next day and find their work where they left it

What we build to get there

  • Real accounts and login. Today the platform resolves one seeded development user for everybody — this is the single biggest piece of work
  • The ten workflow roles, with permissions enforced per project and per stage
  • Write paths throughout. Most screens currently only read; the MVP has to save
  • The 40-table schema applied, so data persists in the shape it will keep
  • Access from outside the Baku office. Today's IP allowlist is a demo control, not a way to reach users
  • A first answer-quality check, so a draft is worth editing rather than distrusting

Deliberately not in it

  • Stage 3 CAPEX pricing — waits on the BoQ item list
  • Designer and vendor access — needs the protected entry point and a test environment
  • Procurement, Aconex and Coins — November
  • The construction control tower
  • Concept Design estimation, to the extent the cost data has not been captured yet

How we will know it worked. Not that it stays up — that someone tells us what is wrong with it. A platform nobody complains about in October is a platform nobody used.

Why this shape matters. The green chain is a complete request path, and it is the part that is expensive to retrofit: a person reaches the portal, the portal reaches the core, the core reaches the database and the model, and the model is reachable only through a gateway that masks. The red sits in two places — the edge, which is what stands between us and the first external designer, and the later stages, which wait on inputs rather than on engineering. The grey is the honest gap: operational ownership has not been decided, and that is a conversation to have before go-live, not after.
